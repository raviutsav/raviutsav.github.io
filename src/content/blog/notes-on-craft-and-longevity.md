A few observations collected over recent months while building tools and reflecting on maintenance:

### 1. Plain Text is Lindy

Anything saved in plain text, standard Markdown, or open relational formats will be legible thirty years from today. The bespoke proprietary data formats of five years ago are already decaying. When in doubt, prefer UTF-8 text files stored in a plain directory over complex cloud silos.

### 2. High-Quality Tools Disappear During Use

Whether it is a Japanese hand plane, a fountain pen, or a well-configured terminal, great tools do not call attention to themselves. They provide frictionless transmission between intention and artifact. The moment a tool forces you to think about its configuration while working, it has broken the spell.

### 3. Maintainability Over Cleverness

Code that looks clever today is an unpaid invoice handed to your future self. The best code looks almost pedestrian—simple variable names, predictable control flow, and zero clever metaprogramming tricks.

> "A craftsman does not boast of how sharp their chisel is; they show the joint that holds two pieces of wood seamlessly together."
